The Short Version

A Mother Flees With Her Children. Then What?

The war in Sudan has forced millions of people from their homes. Many women cross borders alone with their children, arriving with no money, no belongings and no one to share the load. In Uganda, SMIP stands with them.

In brief: single mothers displaced by war need food and shelter first, then healing, income and childcare. SMIP provides all four through its programs.

Why Mothers Are Most At Risk

The Weight A Single Mother Carries

Conflict hits hardest when one adult must do everything.

Lost Home

Houses, land and belongings left behind or destroyed.

No Income

No work, savings or support while children still need to eat.

Trauma

Fear and grief that make daily decisions harder.

Child Safety

Children need protection, health care and school.

Key Terms

Understanding The Words Used In This Crisis

Refugee

A person who crossed an international border to escape conflict or persecution.

Asylum-Seeker

A person who has asked for protection and is waiting for their claim to be decided.

Displaced Person

Someone forced to leave home, inside or outside their own country.

Psychosocial Support

Care that helps people recover emotionally and rebuild social ties.

What SMIP Does

Meeting Urgent Needs, Then Building Independence

  • Emergency food, clothing, hygiene supplies and financial support

Questions

Common Questions About The Crisis

A mother who flees alone carries every responsibility herself: protecting her children, finding food and shelter, and earning an income, often after losing her home, belongings and support network.

A refugee has crossed an international border to escape conflict or persecution. An internally displaced person (IDP) has fled their home but remains inside their own country.

Uganda hosts about two million refugees and asylum-seekers, according to UNHCR Uganda operational data, with women and children making up roughly 77% of the refugee population.
